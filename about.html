Shop Beans

A small roastery, on purpose.

Established 2017
Three locations · Brooklyn
Direct trade · 26 farms

"Coffee should taste like the place it came from — not a marketing copy. Slowness is not a brand value here. It is the only way we know how."

Meridian started in the spring of 2017 in a 600-square-foot space in Bushwick, with one Probat roaster, a stubborn belief about flavour, and a single bag of unroasted Ethiopian Yirgacheffe. The first six months were almost entirely just learning. We opened to the public in November.

Eight years later, the roaster is the same. The beliefs have not changed. We work directly with twenty-six farms across four origin regions, and we still cup every single lot ourselves before it goes to the public. We have grown into three small cafes across the city, but the roastery has stayed at the size it needs to be — small enough that everyone touches every bag.

The cafes are different from each other on purpose. The Bushwick flagship is loud and busy. The Lower East Side one is quiet and deliberate. The Long Island City one is in between. What they share is a counter that does one thing very well, made by people who care.

What we believe

Four commitments

No. 01

Direct, always

No middlemen. No commodity-grade lots. We visit every farm we buy from, twice a year. Farmers on our roster earn 40–80% above commodity price.

No. 02

Roasted to order

No bag in our catalogue is roasted more than 48 hours before it ships. We never store roasted coffee. We never compromise on freshness.

No. 03

Slow on purpose

Filter coffee is brewed individually. Espresso is dialled in twice a day. We hold reservation seats for ninety minutes. None of this scales. None of it is meant to.

How we got here

A quiet chronology

2017

The first bag

Marisol Halvorsen leases a 600-square-foot space in Bushwick. The first commercial bag of Meridian coffee — Ethiopia Yirgacheffe — is roasted in November.

2019

Direct trade begins

First origin trip to Colombia and Costa Rica. We sign our first three direct trade agreements — Llano Bonito, El Paraíso, Tarrazú Honey.

2021

Two more rooms

The Orchard opens on Lower East Side, followed by The Vernon in Long Island City. The roastery stays the same size; the team triples.

2024

The subscription

After three years of customer requests, we launch a national subscription program. Beans roasted to order, shipped within 48 hours. Six thousand subscribers in the first nine months.

2026

Where we are

Twenty-six direct trade farms. Three cafes. Six thousand-plus subscribers. Same roaster. Same beliefs.
